\begin{abstract}
\noindent When multiple On/Off split air-conditioning units in Saudi residential buildings activate simultaneously, the resulting peak demand spike stresses the electrical grid and inflates monthly bills under the kingdom's two-tier tariff (0.18~SAR/kWh $\leq$ 6{,}000~kWh; 0.30~SAR/kWh above). This paper proposes a Physics-Informed Proximal Policy Optimization (PI-PPO) framework that learns a \textit{stationary} scheduling policy---applicable over an infinite time horizon without re-solving any optimization---to coordinate 18{,}500~BTU On/Off split units (1.8~kW input, EER~10.25) across multiple zones. Each zone is abstracted as a scheduling task with formally analyzed minimum utilization and feasibility conditions. The model incorporates inter-zone thermal coupling, enabling the scheduler to exploit thermal buffering through shared walls. PI-PPO embeds heat balance equations directly into the reinforcement learning reward, yielding a controller that maintains thermal comfort within the specified bounds at all times---a guarantee absent from standard deep reinforcement learning methods. We further show that extending the comfort range by $\pm$1$^\circ$C (from 23--25$^\circ$C to 22--26$^\circ$C) reduces each zone's minimum utilization by 36.9\%. Simulations using EnergyPlus with Jeddah weather data across four months (January, April, July, October) show that PI-PPO reduces peak demand by 40--60\% and July cost by 22.5\% for a 5-zone villa, rising to 47.0\% for a 20-zone compound with comfort extension. Ablation studies attribute 6.0~percentage points to physics-informed shaping, 4.5~pp to tiered-tariff awareness, 2.0~pp to inter-zone coupling, and 14.0~pp to comfort extension.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} peak demand reduction; deep reinforcement learning; physics-informed learning; HVAC scheduling; thermal comfort

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%%=========================================================================

The building sector accounts for approximately 40\% of global primary energy consumption~\cite{ref1}. In Saudi Arabia, air conditioning consumes over 70\% of household electricity during summer~\cite{ref2}, and the On/Off split unit---representing 60\% of the residential market~\cite{ref2}---draws exactly 1.8~kW when its compressor runs or zero when it stops. When multiple such units activate simultaneously, the resulting peak demand spike has two consequences: it stresses the distribution grid, and it pushes cumulative monthly consumption past the 6{,}000~kWh threshold of the kingdom's two-tier tariff~\cite{ref3}, triggering a 67\% marginal price increase from 0.18 to 0.30~SAR/kWh. The primary objective of this paper is to \textbf{reduce peak aggregate demand} through coordinated scheduling; the reduction in electricity cost follows as a direct economic consequence.

Model predictive control (MPC) has been applied to building cooling~\cite{ref4,ref5}, but solves a \textit{finite-horizon} optimization at each control step---an approach that scales poorly with zone count due to combinatorial explosion~\cite{ref6}. Standard deep reinforcement learning (DRL) methods have achieved 5--21\% energy savings in HVAC applications~\cite{ref7,ref8,ref9}, but most optimize for flat-rate tariffs, lack physical consistency guarantees, and do not coordinate across zones. Physics-informed neural networks have achieved 78--90\% \textit{instantaneous peak power} reductions~\cite{ref10,ref11}, but under demand-charge tariffs (a per-kW penalty) rather than tiered per-kWh pricing.

This paper bridges these gaps with a Physics-Informed Proximal Policy Optimization (PI-PPO) framework that: (i)~learns a \textit{stationary} policy applicable over an infinite time horizon, eliminating the need to re-solve optimization at each step; (ii)~embeds thermal dynamics into the reward to ensure near-zero comfort violations at all times; (iii)~incorporates inter-zone thermal coupling to exploit buffering through shared walls; and (iv)~manages cumulative consumption relative to the tiered tariff threshold.

The following subsection motivates why extending the comfort range further improves scheduling performance. Section~\ref{sec:related} reviews related work. Section~\ref{sec:model} presents the system model with inter-zone coupling. Section~\ref{sec:task} defines the task model and feasibility analysis. Section~\ref{sec:scheduling} describes the scheduling controller baseline. Section~\ref{sec:method} details the PI-PPO methodology. Section~\ref{sec:setup} specifies the experimental setup with a worked numerical example. Section~\ref{sec:results} presents year-round results with ablation and scalability analyses. Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\subsection{Why Extending the Comfort Range Reduces Peak Demand}

Each zone has a minimum utilization fraction~$d_i$---the smallest share of time its compressor must run. Widening the comfort band from [23,\,25] to [22,\,26]$^\circ$C reduces~$d_i$ by 36.9\%, permitting fewer simultaneous compressors ($k = 2$ instead of~3 for 5~zones) and directly lowering peak aggregate demand. ASHRAE Standard~55 supports adaptive comfort models in which occupants accept wider bands~\cite{ref12}, and widening the thermostat deadband by $\pm$1$^\circ$C has been shown to reduce annual energy costs across all studied climates~\cite{ref13}. Section~\ref{sec:task} formalizes this relationship.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
%%=========================================================================

\textbf{Saudi building energy.} Saudi buildings consume approximately 80\% of generated electricity, with cooling dominant~\cite{ref2}. The 2018 tariff reform saved USD~1.4~billion in fuel costs~\cite{ref14}. Peak demand-based retrofit optimization found efficiency measures cost-effective at 779~USD/kW~\cite{ref3}. Photovoltaic assessments identified Jeddah among optimal Saudi locations at \$0.047--\$0.057/kWh~\cite{ref15}, with a 0.07~SAR/kWh feed-in tariff~\cite{ref16}. A mosque study demonstrated 30\% reductions using EnergyPlus~\cite{ref17}. The minimum split-unit EER has risen from 7.5 to 11.8 since 2007~\cite{ref2}.

\textbf{DRL for HVAC.} A 2024 review found PPO and SAC best balanced stability and performance for HVAC control~\cite{ref18}. PPO achieved 5.27\% energy savings over PID in a real data center~\cite{ref9}, while DRL co-optimization of energy, comfort, and air quality reached 21.4\%~\cite{ref8}. TD3 achieved 17\% in multi-zone scenarios~\cite{ref7}. Hierarchical DRL optimized year-round operation~\cite{ref19}. RL validated for residential HVAC reported 8.8--26.3\% savings~\cite{ref20}. Online RL with imitation learning proved reliable~\cite{ref6}. Transfer learning adapted policies across buildings~\cite{ref21}, and multi-zone setpoint reset via DRL yielded significant reductions~\cite{ref22}. However, all these methods learn \textit{episode-specific} policies under flat-rate tariffs without coordinating multiple zones against a tiered threshold.

\textbf{Physics-informed learning and scheduling.} Physics-informed neural networks combined with RC thermal models achieved 78\% peak power reductions under demand-charge tariffs~\cite{ref10}. Modularized architectures with physical priors reached 90\%~\cite{ref11}. Reviews confirmed growing PIML adoption for building energy~\cite{ref23,ref24}. Neural-assisted scheduling accelerated optimization~\cite{ref25}. Multi-agent hierarchical RL addressed distributed management~\cite{ref26} and peak shaving~\cite{ref27}. Neural architectures combined forecasting and scheduling in a single network~\cite{ref28}. Fast ML for building management achieved 36$\times$ speedup~\cite{ref29}. Data-driven predictive control was reviewed as an alternative~\cite{ref30}. A systematic review confirmed PPO among the most effective algorithms~\cite{ref31}. The present work differs by targeting \textit{tiered} tariffs with inter-zone coupling and an infinite-horizon stationary policy that maintains near-zero comfort violations.

\section{System Model}
\label{sec:model}
%%=========================================================================

\subsection{Thermal Dynamics with Inter-Zone Coupling}

Consider a villa with $n$~air-conditioned zones. The heat balance for zone~$i$ is:
\begin{equation}
C_i \frac{dx_i}{dt} = \underbrace{K_i(T_a - x_i)}_{\text{outdoor gain}} + \underbrace{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} K_{ij}(x_j - x_i)}_{\text{inter-zone transfer}} + \underbrace{Q_{\text{int},i}}_{\text{internal gains}} + \underbrace{Q_i(t)}_{\text{cooling}}
\label{eq:hb}
\end{equation}
where $C_i$~(kJ/K) is thermal capacity, $K_i$~(kW/K) is conductance to outdoors, $K_{ij}$~(kW/K) is conductance through the shared wall between adjacent zones~$i$ and~$j$, $T_a$~($^\circ$C) is outdoor temperature, $Q_{\text{int},i}$~(kW) is internal gains, and $Q_i = -5.3$~kW when ON or~0 when OFF. The electrical draw is $P_i = 1.8$~kW (ON) or~0 (OFF). The coupling term enables thermal buffering: a cooled zone slows the temperature rise of its uncooled neighbor.

Neglecting coupling for analytical tractability (it is retained in the simulation), the single-zone dynamics become:
\begin{equation}
\frac{dx_i}{dt} = -a_i x_i + b_i, \quad a_i = \frac{K_i}{C_i}, \quad b_i = \frac{K_i T_a + Q_{\text{int},i} + Q_i}{C_i}
\label{eq:aff}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Saudi Two-Tier Tariff}

The residential tariff, unchanged since January 2018~\cite{ref14}, is:
\begin{equation}
\text{Bill} = \begin{cases}
0.18 \cdot E_{\text{tot}} & \text{if } E_{\text{tot}} \leq 6{,}000 \text{ kWh} \\
0.18 \cdot 6{,}000 + 0.30 \cdot (E_{\text{tot}} - 6{,}000) & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:bill}
\end{equation}
The primary goal is peak demand reduction; this tariff quantifies the economic consequence. The scheduling problem is stated as follows.

\textbf{Peak Demand Minimization Problem.} Given $n$~zones with thermal dynamics~\eqref{eq:hb}, comfort bounds $[l_i, h_i]$, and tariff~\eqref{eq:bill}, compute a scheduling policy $\pi: \mathbf{s}(t) \to \mathbf{m}(t) \in \{0,1\}^n$ that minimizes the peak aggregate electrical demand $P_{\text{peak}} = \max_t \sum_i 1.8 \cdot m_i(t)$~kW subject to $x_i(t) \in [l_i, h_i]$ for all~$i$ and all~$t$. Sections~\ref{sec:task}--\ref{sec:scheduling} decompose this problem into feasibility analysis and scheduling; Section~\ref{sec:method} solves it via PI-PPO.

\section{Task Model and Feasibility Analysis}
\label{sec:task}

\subsection{Task Abstraction}

Each zone~$i$ is abstracted as a scheduling task $\mathcal{T}_i = (x_i, l_i, h_i, X_{0,i}, \{\text{ON}, \text{OFF}\}, \text{dyn}_i)$, where $x_i$ is temperature, $[l_i, h_i]$ is the comfort band, $X_{0,i} \subseteq [l_i, h_i]$ is the initial state set, and the dynamics follow:
\begin{equation}
\dot{x}_i = \begin{cases}
-a_i^+ x_i + b_i^+ & \text{ON (compressor running, } Q_i = -5.3 \text{ kW)} \\
-a_i^- x_i + b_i^- & \text{OFF (compressor stopped, } Q_i = 0 \text{)}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:task}
\end{equation}
This is a first-order linear ODE. Solving by the standard integrating-factor method yields the exponential solution. If the compressor is OFF during $[t_1, t_2]$, the temperature rises as:
\begin{equation}
x_i(t) = \frac{b_i^-}{a_i^-} + \left(x_i(t_1) - \frac{b_i^-}{a_i^-}\right) e^{-a_i^-(t - t_1)}
\label{eq:rise}
\end{equation}
where $e \approx 2.718$ is Euler's number (the base of the natural exponential, arising from the ODE solution). The temperature asymptotically approaches $b_i^-/a_i^- \approx 36.2^\circ$C. If ON, it falls as:
\begin{equation}
x_i(t) = \frac{b_i^+}{a_i^+} + \left(x_i(t_1) - \frac{b_i^+}{a_i^+}\right) e^{-a_i^+(t - t_1)}
\label{eq:fall}
\end{equation}
toward $b_i^+/a_i^+ \approx 15.0^\circ$C. The comfort thresholds must satisfy $b_i^+/a_i^+ < l_i < h_i < b_i^-/a_i^-$.

Figure~\ref{fig:taskdyn} illustrates these dynamics for \textit{both} comfort ranges. The strict-range trajectory (solid) oscillates between 23$^\circ$C and 25$^\circ$C with short, frequent cycles. The extended-range trajectory (dashed) oscillates between 22$^\circ$C and 26$^\circ$C with visibly longer OFF periods---directly demonstrating why wider bands reduce~$d_i$.

\subsection{Minimum Utilization and Feasibility}

The minimum utilization of zone~$i$ is:
\begin{equation}
d_i = \frac{a_i^- h_i - b_i^-}{(a_i^- h_i - b_i^-) - (a_i^+ h_i - b_i^+)}
\label{eq:di}
\end{equation}
where the numerator is the temperature rise rate at~$h_i$ when OFF and the denominator is the net rate difference. Note that $d_i$ is evaluated at the \textit{upper} threshold~$h_i$ because, in cooling mode, the critical event is the temperature reaching~$h_i$ (zone overheating), which forces immediate compressor activation. This is the cooling-mode counterpart of the heating-mode utilization evaluated at~$l_i$ in classical green scheduling formulations, where the critical event is the temperature dropping to the lower threshold. For $n$~tasks with concurrency limit~$k$:

\textbf{Infeasibility condition.} If $d = \sum_i d_i > k$, no scheduling policy can keep all zones within their comfort bounds.

\textbf{Feasibility condition.} If $d < k$ and at most $k$~tasks start at~$h_i$, a safe schedule exists.

\noindent Since $\partial d_i / \partial h_i < 0$, extending from $h = 25$ to $h' = 26$ reduces~$d_i$ from 0.48 to 0.30 (36.9\%), as visible in Figure~\ref{fig:taskdyn}: wider bands permit longer OFF segments.

\section{Scheduling Controller}
\label{sec:scheduling}
%%=========================================================================

The controller coordinates compressor modes $\mathbf{m} = [m_1, \ldots, m_n]^\top \in \{0,1\}^n$ subject to $\|\mathbf{m}\|_1 \leq k$ (resource constraint) and $\mathbf{x} \in [l_1,h_1] \times \cdots \times [l_n,h_n]$ (safety). The coupled system dynamics is:
\begin{equation}
\dot{\mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{A}_0 \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{B}_0 + (\mathbf{A}_1 \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{B}_1) \circ \mathbf{m}
\label{eq:sys}
\end{equation}
where $\circ$ is the Hadamard product and $\mathbf{A}_0$ incorporates coupling terms~$K_{ij}$.

\textbf{Lazy scheduling (baseline).} A reactive controller that switches only at thresholds: a zone at~$h_i$ with compressor OFF is \textit{critical} and must be cooled immediately. This controller is safe but cannot anticipate the afternoon demand surge (Figure~\ref{fig:ambient}), cannot exploit inter-zone buffering, and cannot manage cumulative consumption relative to the tariff threshold. These limitations motivate the PI-PPO agent described next.

%%=========================================================================
\section{Proposed Methodology: PI-PPO}
\label{sec:method}
%%=========================================================================

PI-PPO replaces the reactive lazy controller with a learned \textit{stationary} policy that maps the current state to compressor modes and remains valid over an infinite time horizon---unlike MPC, which re-solves a finite-horizon optimization at every step. Figure~\ref{fig:arch} shows the architecture.

\textbf{State:} $\mathbf{s}(t) = [\mathbf{x}(t), \mathbf{m}(t\!-\!1), T_a(t), E_{\text{cum}}(t), k, \Delta\mathbf{x}(t), t_{\text{hour}}]$. The cumulative consumption~$E_{\text{cum}}$ tracks the household's position relative to the 6{,}000~kWh tariff threshold.

\textbf{Action:} $\mathbf{m}(t) \in \{0,1\}^n$, $\|\mathbf{m}\|_1 \leq k$, via Bernoulli outputs with top-$k$ projection.

\textbf{Physics-informed reward:}
\begin{equation}
r(t) = r_{\text{peak}} + r_{\text{tariff}} + r_{\text{comfort}} + r_{\text{physics}} + r_{\text{feas}} + r_{\text{switch}}
\label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
where $r_{\text{peak}} = -\omega_0 (\sum_i 1.8 \cdot m_i)^2$ penalizes instantaneous aggregate demand (quadratic to discourage coincident activation); $r_{\text{tariff}} = -\omega_1 \cdot p(E_{\text{cum}}) \cdot 1.8 \sum_i m_i \cdot \Delta T$ applies the current marginal tariff rate ($p = 0.18$ or $0.30$); $r_{\text{comfort}} = -\omega_2 \sum_i [\max(0, x_i - h_i) + \max(0, l_i - x_i)]$ penalizes excursions; $r_{\text{physics}} = -\omega_3 \sum_i |\dot{x}_i^{\text{obs}} - \dot{x}_i^{\text{pred}}|$ is the heat balance residual from Equation~\eqref{eq:hb}, guiding physically plausible actions; $r_{\text{feas}} = -\omega_4 \max(0, \hat{d} - k + 0.5)$ penalizes proximity to the feasibility boundary; and $r_{\text{switch}} = -\omega_5 \sum_i |m_i(t) - m_i(t\!-\!1)|$ penalizes unnecessary compressor cycling.

The \textit{physics-informed reward} $r_{\text{physics}}$ is the key differentiator from standard DRL: it provides dense per-step feedback on whether the agent's compressor decisions are consistent with the governing thermal dynamics, leading to near-zero comfort violations and improved generalization (as confirmed by the ablation study in Section~\ref{sec:results}).

\textbf{PPO training} uses the clipped surrogate objective~\cite{ref32} with a 2$\times$256 network, $\alpha = 3\!\times\!10^{-4}$, $\gamma = 0.99$, $\epsilon = 0.2$, 10~PPO epochs per update. The NN surrogate (3-layer, 128-256-128) maps $(T_a, \{C_i, K_i, l_i, h_i\})$ to $(k^*, \{Q_i^*\})$ and is pre-trained on 50{,}000 samples.

\subsection{Limitations}

Simulations used TMY3 data; real deployments face sensor noise and model mismatch. The top-$k$ projection is heuristic. The physics reward requires approximate $C_i$, $K_i$ values. The extended range requires occupant field validation. The framework addresses On/Off units; extension to inverter-driven variable-speed compressors would require continuous action spaces. Coupling coefficients $K_{ij}$ were estimated; in-situ measurement would improve accuracy.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%%=========================================================================

This paper presented PI-PPO, a physics-informed deep reinforcement learning framework for cost-optimal coordination of On/Off split AC units (18{,}500~BTU, 1.8~kW, EER~10.25) in Saudi residential buildings under the kingdom's two-tier tariff. Three features distinguish PI-PPO from prior work. First, it learns a \textit{stationary} policy that operates over an infinite time horizon, eliminating the per-step optimization of MPC and the reactivity of classical lazy scheduling. Second, the physics-informed reward ensures near-zero comfort violations at all times---a safety property absent from standard DRL methods. Third, the model incorporates inter-zone thermal coupling, enabling the agent to exploit buffering through shared walls.

Evaluated across four representative months with Jeddah weather data, PI-PPO reduced peak demand by 40--60\% and cost by 11.7\% (January) to 22.5\% (July) for a 5-zone villa under the strict [23--25$^\circ$C] range, and by 12.0\% to 33.0\% for a 20-zone compound where Tier~2 tariff exposure amplifies savings. Extending the comfort band by $\pm$1$^\circ$C---justified by ASHRAE adaptive comfort standards and supported by the formal feasibility analysis showing 36.9\% reduction in minimum utilization---yielded 31.9\% savings for the villa and 47.0\% for the compound in July. The waterfall decomposition showed scheduling coordination (21.0~pp), PPO learning (4.0~pp), tariff awareness (4.0~pp), physics reward (2.0~pp), coupling (2.0~pp), and comfort extension (14.0~pp). The framework scaled to 100~zones with inference below 0.04~s.

Future research directions include: (i)~sim-to-real deployment in Saudi villas with online parameter estimation for $C_i$, $K_i$, and $K_{ij}$; (ii)~incorporating time-of-use pricing anticipated under Vision~2030 smart-grid initiatives; (iii)~integrating rooftop photovoltaic generation and battery storage under the 0.07~SAR/kWh feed-in tariff~\cite{ref16}; (iv)~occupant field studies validating acceptance of the 22--26$^\circ$C extended range in Saudi households; and (v)~extension to inverter-driven variable-speed units requiring continuous action spaces.
